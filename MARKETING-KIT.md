# Launchstead marketing kit

Launchstead is in private early access. This kit is for honest, one-to-one outreach to freelancers and small agencies that build and host client websites. These drafts are for editing and sending by the operator. They have not been sent.

## Positioning

**Primary audience:** Freelancers and small agencies shipping brochure sites, marketing sites, and small Node.js applications for clients.

**Secondary audience:** Indie developers who want a simple place to release their own projects.

**Problem:** Client work often leaves the builder carrying a messy chain of hosting steps, release context, and handoff details. Small teams need a clearer way to move from project files to a site they can review and maintain.

**Promise:** Launchstead is a hosting workspace that helps you bring a static site or Node.js app to the web, keep releases easy to follow, and keep client projects organised while the product is still taking shape.

**Why now:** Launchstead is inviting a small number of people into private early access to learn how client work moves from build to handoff. Access is arranged directly. There is no public signup, public pricing, live payment flow, domain purchasing, SLA, database hosting, Git integration, or Docker workflow promised by this page.

**One-line description:** A hosting workspace for freelancers and small agencies shipping client sites.

## Onboarding interview questions

Start by asking for a recent client project and let the person describe the process in their own words. Use these prompts as needed:

1. What kinds of websites do you build most often, and who maintains them after handoff?
2. Walk me through the last client site from finished code to a public URL.
3. Which parts of that process are manual, repetitive, or easy to forget?
4. Where do release notes, approvals, previews, and rollback decisions live today?
5. What does a client need to see before you call a site ready?
6. How do you separate projects and access when several clients are in flight?
7. What happens when a client asks what changed or asks you to restore an earlier version?
8. Which hosting capabilities do you depend on today, and which are optional?
9. What would make you trust a new hosting workflow with a non-critical client project?
10. What would make you stop using it after the first project?
11. If Launchstead supported your current workflow, where would it save time or reduce context switching?
12. What would you expect to pay for hosting once the product is ready, and what would you need to know first?

## Interview notes

Record the project type, current hosting path, handoff owner, repeated pain points, and exact language used. Connecting existing domains with DNS verification and HTTPS is available. Project-scoped read-only client access, email invitations, owner-visible access history and internal portfolio labels are available. New recipients still require operator-approved registration. Do not promise domain purchases, live payments, uptime commitments, ownership transfer or integrations that have not shipped.

## Assisted first-site checklist

- Confirm static or supported Node.js fit, dependencies, storage needs, domain ownership and current traffic.
- Agree on the private-access limits and identify who maintains the site.
- Arrange allowlisted access, then create the website and upload its ZIP without secrets.
- Build if needed, publish, and connect the existing domain using the displayed DNS records.
- Confirm HTTPS, key pages, and one successful content update.
- Use a client label to organize the portfolio. Explain that labels do not grant access.
- For client review, invite the approved account through Clients and confirm it sees only its shared website. Explain that Team membership gives workspace-wide access.
- Show how to revoke client access and review recent access changes.
- Record time to first live site, operator help required and the next requested feature.
- Ask for explicit agreement before sharing names, screenshots or a case study.
